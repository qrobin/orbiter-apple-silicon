# Planetary maps

The Sun, Mercury, Venus atmosphere and surface, Moon, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn,
Uranus, Neptune and Saturn-ring maps are by **Solar System Scope / INOVE**,
used under **Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0)**:
https://www.solarsystemscope.com/textures/ and
https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/. Earth uses the separately
credited NASA 8K image. Mercury, Venus surface, Moon and Mars use the
8192 × 4096 originals; the Saturn rings use the 8192 × 500 original. Sun,
Venus atmosphere, Jupiter and Saturn are 4096 × 2048, and Uranus and
Neptune are 2048 × 1024. The source's files labelled 8K for the Sun,
Jupiter and Saturn actually have 4096 × 2048 pixels.
Solar System Scope notes that its maps contain enhanced color and invented
fill where observations are incomplete.

Moon textures for Phobos, Deimos, Io, Europa, Ganymede, Callisto, Mimas,
Enceladus, Tethys, Dione, Rhea, Iapetus, Ariel, Umbriel, Titania, Oberon,
Miranda and Triton derive from NASA/JPL/Caltech/USGS Solar System Simulator
mosaics: https://maps.jpl.nasa.gov/tmaps/. Original mosaics are generally
1440 × 720, based on Voyager and in some cases Galileo imagery. They were
resampled to 2048 × 1024 and tinted for display. Large missing areas in some
maps use blended, transformed Phobos imagery as artistic filler; this does
not depict actual geography. Resampling does not add observed detail.

Titan is based on a Cassini ISS mosaic, via
https://github.com/Sanghel/solar-system-3d/tree/develop/public/textures/satellites;
the source is a 1024 × 512 NASA/USGS-derived near-infrared map, resampled and
tinted for display. Vesta uses Dawn Framing Camera HAMO mosaic tiles from
NASA/JPL Vesta Trek: https://trek.nasa.gov/tiles/apidoc/trekAPI.html?body=vesta.
Hyperion uses a resampled, tinted shaded-relief map by Philip Stooke from the
NASA PDS archive: https://sbnarchive.psi.edu/pds3/non_mission/EAR_A_3_RDR_STOOKEMAPS_V1_0/maps/.
Proteus and Nereid are fictional stand-ins made from transformed Phobos
imagery because complete maps were not available; their markings are not
observations of those moons.

All close-range solid-body materials add procedural detail. This detail and
the elevation geometry are visual approximations, not scientific data.
